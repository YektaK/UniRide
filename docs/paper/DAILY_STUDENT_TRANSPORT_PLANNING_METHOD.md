## 1. Problem setting

A single campus provides transport for students with weekly class timetables. For a selected service date, the corresponding weekday determines each student's first class start and last class end. An eligible student generates a pickup leg before the first class and a drop-off leg after the last class. These are separate demand occurrences: one student can contribute two daily passenger trips. Students without an applicable class are excluded from that day's timetable demand.

Passengers use two separate capacity pools: wheelchair positions (Sw) and seated positions (So). An empty seated position cannot replace a wheelchair position. Each vehicle has capacities for both types. Each route starts and ends at the campus depot, serves its assigned demand occurrences once, and respects both capacities. Occurrence identifiers distinguish passengers even when they share a physical stop.

Admission is an input to planning. A hypothetical scenario can treat pending legs as admitted while retaining cancellations. This preview is explicitly non-publishable and does not create confirmations. Operational demand and a hypothetical timetable scenario must be reported separately: their passenger sets and inferred fleet requirements can differ.

## 2. Travel-time data

Let c(i,j) denote travel time in minutes from physical location i to location j. Planning uses a stored, directed matrix derived from road-network routing. [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:95-98] c(i,j) may differ from c(j,i). [S: docs/designs/EXACT_SOLVER_AMPL_DESIGN.md:20] Straight-line distances and coordinate-based travel-time approximations are not used in this operational method. [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:97-98] Missing or invalid required arcs prevent certification rather than being silently assigned zero travel time. [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:348-355]

The matrix is not assumed to satisfy the triangle inequality. [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:167-168] A direct campus-to-stop time is therefore not a certified lower bound on a route reaching that stop through other stops. [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:167-168] A wave cannot be rejected solely because a direct arc exceeds its ride limit. [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:167-169] Different passenger occurrences at one physical location may have a zero connecting hop; this does not justify zero travel between different locations. [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:125-127] [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:348-355]

A matrix snapshot is captured before solving. [S: src/services/daily-plan-run.ts:522-539] Wave requests are bound to its identity, and route verification uses the captured authoritative arc values. ## 3. Demand waves and constraints

Demands are grouped by direction and exact anchor time. A pickup anchor is the first class start minus a 15-minute arrival buffer: a 09:00 class produces an 08:45 campus arrival anchor. Drop-off departure is anchored at the last class end plus a 15-minute departure buffer. Groups within the same clock hour but at different anchors remain separate waves. The current method does not solve individual passenger time windows within a wave.

For a closed route D, v1, …, vk, D, tour duration is the sum of all directed arcs, including the empty outbound or return journey. This sum must not exceed the tour cap T. Passenger ride time has a separate definition:

- Pickup: a passenger boarding at vi experiences the arc sum from vi through the remaining stops to D. The empty initial journey from D is excluded.
- Drop-off: a passenger alighting at vi experiences the arc sum from D through preceding stops to vi. The final return to D is excluded.

Every passenger ride must be at most R. These definitions include travel only; boarding time and waiting are not added in the current anchored-wave calculation. Capacity is checked separately for Sw and So. Defaults are R = 90 minutes and T = 150 minutes, with selectable ranges of 15–240 and 30–300 minutes respectively. These are different service constraints.

For identical vehicles with capacities qSw and qSo, a wave containing nSw and nSo passengers has the necessary seat-capacity floor

**Lseat = max(⌈nSw / qSw⌉, ⌈nSo / qSo⌉).**

This floor ignores ride limits, tour limits, and inter-wave reuse. It is a minimum imposed by seats, not the number of feasible routes or a proof of the minimum daily fleet. Its maximum across the day's waves is also a necessary daily lower bound.

## 4. Solution method

### 4.1 Route-first, cluster-second search

Each wave uses a genetic algorithm (GA) over a permutation of passenger occurrences, called a giant tour. [S: src/services/daily-plan-run.ts:526-554] [S: uniride_core/algorithms/ga_split_engine.py:97-120] A split decoder partitions consecutive subsequences into depot-closed vehicle routes. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/string_split_decoder.py:324-367] [S: uniride_core/algorithms/string_split_decoder.py:249-269] This follows the route-first, cluster-second representation associated with [Prins (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0305-0548(03)00158-8). [S: optimizer_api/strategies/ga_split_strategy.py:44-55] The present implementation has its own constraints and acceptance checks; no comparative performance claim is inferred from the reference. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/string_split_decoder.py:337-367] [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:333-358]

Initialization combines randomized permutations and a nearest-neighbour tour. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/ga_split_engine.py:71-94] Tournament selection, order crossover, mutation, and elitism generate new candidates. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/ga_split_engine.py:251-293] Periodic local search modifies a selected giant tour, which is then decoded and evaluated again. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/ga_split_engine.py:352-370] The outer ranking prioritizes feasibility, then route count, then total travel time. [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:173]

The decoder enumerates feasible contiguous segments under both passenger capacities and the tour and ride limits. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/string_split_decoder.py:324-367] Dynamic programming chooses a minimum-travel-time partition for a fixed permutation, preferring partitions without violations. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/string_split_decoder.py:202-228] This restricted calculation does not optimize all permutations, and its cost objective differs from the outer GA's route-count priority. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/string_split_decoder.py:190-228] [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:173] Neither a decoded partition nor the final wave result proves globally minimum route count. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/string_split_decoder.py:202-228] [S: uniride_core/algorithms/ga_split_engine.py:318-379] With nonmetric travel, a duration violation also does not imply that every longer segment violates the limit; alternatives must still be examined. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/string_split_decoder.py:350-358] [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:167-168]

The single-type daily planner selects `ga_split` and supplies no per-request algorithm configuration; the heterogeneous campaign additionally calls `ga_split_hf` to build quota options. ### 4.2 Independent certification

An independent certificate reconstructs every route using the captured matrix. [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:348-358] It checks required occurrence coverage, continuity, depot closure, passenger counts and capacities, tour duration, and direction-specific ride limits. [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:333-355] [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:660-684] A 0.005-minute tolerance per reported arc accommodates two-decimal formatting, with corresponding aggregate formatting checks; it is not extra operational ride allowance. [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:129-140] [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:348-355] [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:339-341] Solver-reported durations alone do not establish feasibility. [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:348-355] The preview also checks whether accepted output can form consistent daily route jobs. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:887-925]

Only accepted routes proceed to vehicle assignment. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:911-932] A failed certificate establishes that the returned candidate is unacceptable. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:918-925] Failure to generate a candidate or exhaustion of a search budget does not prove that no feasible routing exists. [S: uniride_core/algorithms/string_split_decoder.py:236-247] [S: uniride_core/algorithms/ga_split_engine.py:318-379] A feasibility certificate is not an optimality certificate. [S: optimizer_api/verification/response_certifier.py:357-358]

### 4.3 Vehicle reuse across waves

A pickup route with duration t and arrival anchor a becomes the interval [a−t, a]; a drop-off route becomes [a, a+t]. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:489-494] Intervals outside the service day are rejected. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:495-502] A vehicle can serve multiple routes if both capacity pools suffice and each next route starts no earlier than the preceding route's end plus its cooldown. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:623-634]

Daily assignment searches for the fewest distinct vehicles for these fixed route jobs. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:829-877] It explores assignments in time order, uses concurrency lower bounds, and avoids equivalent branches for vehicles with matching capacities, cooldowns, and availability. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:724-742] [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:784-826] The default budget is 50,000 visited nodes. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:6] [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:745-748] [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:829-835] A completed search, or a feasible witness meeting a valid lower bound, can prove the minimum for the given routes. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:829-877] [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:197-198] If the budget is exhausted before proof, the result is indeterminate; an available witness gives only an upper bound. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:829-877] [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:197-198] Completed failure establishes a shortage for the fixed routes and candidate fleet, not for every alternative routing. [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:866-877] [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:203-204]

For identical virtual vehicles this provides a conditional fleet minimum after heuristic routing. [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:197-198] [S: docs/paper/results/week-2026-10-05-4sw5so/README.md:26] [S: src/services/dudullu-preview.ts:246-256] It is not a joint minimum over all possible routes. [S: docs/paper/results/week-2026-10-05-4sw5so/README.md:26] Comparing its count with a heterogeneous real fleet's vehicle count is insufficient: physical capacities, cooldowns, and assignment feasibility require separate evidence. [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:203-204]

## 7. Further study

Heterogeneous routing with Sw/So pools is now represented by the archived scenarios, while a joint optimum over all route sets and vehicle types remains outside their evidence. [S: docs/paper/results/week-2026-10-05-fleet/README.md:13-43] Matrix-based clustering remains a separate research question; geometric grouping does not replace road-network evaluation. [S: docs/DECISION_LOG.md:119-123]

[S: docs/designs/EXACT_SOLVER_AMPL_DESIGN.md:201-202] Each solver call (stage 1, stage 2 and the free-duration-bound solve) has a 60-second wall limit, or 600 seconds on peak waves; a peak is the largest-leg wave per date, including ties. [S: docs/designs/EXACT_SOLVER_AMPL_DESIGN.md:86] The archive census contains 57 waves and 226 legs per R, with 50 waves of 1–6 legs and a largest wave of 20; the five peak pickup waves are anchored at 08:45 and contain 20, 11, 11, 10 and 13 legs.
